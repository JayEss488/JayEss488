How these numbers were made

  • Bees per kilometre. One walk is one visit, and counts the full transect (2.0 km) as distance walked, because that is what the survey protocol asks you to do. Nationally this gives about 14 bumblebees per km at the 2015 peak, 12 in 2023 and 9 in 2024 — the scale the Trust reports.
  • Checked against the published totals. For 2025 this dataset gives 53,869 records across 1,109 transects from 984 recorders, and 173,774 bees in total — against 53,867 records, 1,109 transects, 984 BeeWalkers and 173,773 bees as published by the Trust. The underlying figures agree.
  • On the denominator. A section walked with nothing seen leaves no row in the data, so counting only the sections that produced records would drop those sections from the distance and push every rate up by roughly 1.8×. That alternative is available in this tool, but it is not used here.
  • Look-alike species. Buff-tailed and white-tailed workers cannot be told apart in the field, and 32% of all bumblebees recorded nationally cannot be pinned to a single species. Rather than drop them or quietly assign them, the look-alikes are pooled as Bombus terrestris/lucorum agg. — applied to your data and the national figures identically, so both sides of every comparison are counted the same way.
  • Honeybees and solitary bees (2,723 and 0 here) are counted but kept out of every bumblebee rate. 46 bees identified only to genus are in the totals but not attributed to a species.
  • The baseline is the 2010-2024 mean, the comparison used in the Trust’s own annual report.
